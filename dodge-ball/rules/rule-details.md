# Dodge-ball Rule Details

## 1. Field, goals, and ball

The playing area is an expanded soccer field with clearly marked touchlines,
goal lines, center line, center circle, penalty areas, and goal safety zones.
Goals extend across the full width of each goal line. A 10-foot regulation ball
is used for all matches.

The goal safety zone is the marked area immediately in front of each goal.
It protects drivers during high-speed scoring plays and does not create a goalie
position. A defending car may cross the zone only to make a legal, continuous
play on the ball.

## 2. Teams and eligibility

Each team registers eight vehicles and fields five at a time. Any Dodge car or
Dodge-badged truck, from any model year, may play if it passes event inspection
and is approved by officials. Teams may name reserve vehicles and substitute
only at referee-approved stoppages. A substituted vehicle must
leave the field before its replacement enters.

## 3. Match timing

A regulation match consists of two 20-minute halves, separated by a 10-minute
halftime. The referee may stop the clock for safety, vehicle recovery,
inspection, or other significant interruptions. Time added for these stoppages
is announced before the end of each half.

League matches may end in a draw. Elimination matches use two 10-minute
extra-time periods. If tied afterward, teams take alternating ball-off attempts
from the center line until one team leads after five attempts each; sudden death
follows if necessary.

## 4. Starting and restarting play

Each half begins with a center-field kickoff. The team that concedes a goal
restarts from the center field after that goal. At kickoff, all non-kicking cars
remain outside the center circle until the ball is put into play.

The referee restarts play with a drop ball after an unassigned stoppage. A free
drive restarts play after a foul. The attacking team starts its free drive with
the ball stationary at the marked spot; defending cars must give the required
clearance set by the officials.

## 5. Scoring and the ball in play

A goal is scored when the entire ball crosses the opposing goal line between
the marked goal boundaries. The ball remains live after rebound contact with a
goal structure or boundary barrier unless the referee stops play.

When the ball crosses a touchline, the opposing team restarts it from the point
where it left the field. When it crosses a goal line without scoring, the team
that did not last touch the ball restarts from its defensive end.

## 6. Legal play

Cars may use controlled, forward-motion contact to advance, redirect, shield,
or clear the ball. Players may use positioning and formations to create a lane,
but must leave space for opponents to contest the ball safely. The ball may not
be intentionally trapped against a barrier or under a vehicle.

There is no goalie. Every active car is subject to the same field, contact, and
goal safety-zone rules.

## 7. Fouls and discipline

A foul is called for avoidable vehicle-to-vehicle contact away from the ball,
ramming, sideswiping, rear-ending, pinning an opponent, intentional spinning,
or any maneuver the referee considers unsafe. A car may not use the goal safety
zone to block an opponent without making a continuous play on the ball.

Ordinary fouls result in a free drive. Serious or repeated fouls may result in a
yellow card. A red card removes the driver and car from the match; the team
continues with four cars unless event officials authorize a safety replacement.
Officials may halt play immediately for unsafe equipment or driving.

## 8. Officials and safety

The referee has final authority over starts, stoppages, goals, fouls, vehicle
eligibility during a match, and safety. Event staff inspect each car before play
and may remove any car that no longer meets requirements. Drivers must follow
the venue's safety procedures and all official instructions.
